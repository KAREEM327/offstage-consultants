The uncomfortable part

Most teams score Band 2. Most believe they're Band 4.

The gap is almost always the same thing. Your controls live in prose — a policy doc, a README, an instruction in the system prompt. Nothing refuses a violation.

A model can ignore an instruction. A feature shipped six months from now can route around an application-layer check without its author ever knowing the check existed. That is not a hypothetical: it is the documented reason a production agent platform added a database constraint only after an unrelated feature silently mis-attributed audited runs.

A model cannot ignore a CHECK constraint. The assessment's entire job is to separate the controls you have written down from the controls that are actually enforced.

What you get

Two documents. One answer.

01 — The Assessment

22 controls across invocation authorization, delegation, tool access, cost containment, observability, and evidence. Each scored 0 (absent) / 1 (documented) / 2 (enforced). Produces a band from 1 to 5 and a fix list ordered by blast radius, not by score.

~45 minutes.

02 — The Conformance Spec

The implementable requirement set behind every control. MUST / SHOULD / LATER, with the actual SQL, schema shapes, and the rationale for each — including which real-world failure produced it. Every requirement maps back to an assessment control, so a failed score tells you precisely what to go build.

Six controls are hard gates. They are fail-open escalation paths — a violation grants privilege rather than merely failing to record it. Fail one and your band is capped regardless of your total. Most maturity models let a strong observability score paper over a broken authorization path. This one refuses to.
